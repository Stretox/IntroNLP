Mesopotamia, Egypt and China
In Mesopotamian mythology, Inanna, goddess of fertility, love, the seasons and harvest, after wanting to marry Enkimdu, a farmer god, against her brother, sun god Utu's will, is petitioned by Dumuzi, the shepherd god, to marry him instead. In his petition Dumuzi compared between his own products: rich milk, yogurt, butter (or ghee) honey cheese and small cheeses, with those of the farmer: bread beans and dates, and concluded with the argument that he produces so much dairy that the human farmer could live off the surplus, Inanna was ultimately convinced and married Dumuzi. In another myth Inanna promised Dumuzi that she would preserve his storehouse in return for provisions of fresh milk, cream and cheese. As a result, cheese figured prominently in the long lived sacred marriage rite of the Dumuzi-Inanna cult, in which the king of Uruk, symbolizing Dumuzi, supplied Inanna, probably represented by a priestess, with cheese and cream, thereby securing her blessing for the following year. This rite was adopted by additional Sumerian cities such as Nippur, Ur and Isin, and evolved over time and place into different forms. It became the Akkadian sacred marriage rites between Nanna and Ningal, developed into that of Ningirsu and Bab, and Nanse and Nindar, and eventually into the Semitic-Akkadian rite of Tammuz-Ishtar which persisted into the common era. Numerous seals and tablets found in Inanna's temple serve as testimony to the annual quotas of cheese and butter herders were required to deliver to the goddess.
